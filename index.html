Kansas

More Kansas children lack health insurance than the national average

About 8.5% of Kansas children under 19 had no health insurance in 2025, up from 7% in 2024 and 5.2% in 2022, according to Census Bureau data reported by the Kansas Reflector. The national rate was 6.2%, making this the second straight year Kansas was higher. Erica Andrade of El Centro said fear of immigration enforcement led some families to drop coverage for eligible children.

One source: Only the Kansas Reflector’s report, carried by KCUR, opened. KAKE’s story on the same figures returned an error twice.

KCUR (Kansas Reflector)

Context

Medicaid and CHIP are government programs that pay for health care for many children in lower-income families. Anne Dwyer of Georgetown University said enrollment in them fell nationwide in 2025, and linked the rise in uninsured children to red tape, federal policy changes and program cuts. Sonder Crane of the Kansas chapter of the American Academy of Pediatrics said uninsured children are more likely to miss checkups and vaccines.

Israel’s president picks Netanyahu rival Eisenkot to form a government

President Isaac Herzog on Saturday gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot the first chance to form a governing coalition, AP and Fox report. Parties holding 67 of parliament’s 120 seats recommended him, Fox reports. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu called for a “national unity government” in a video statement, AP reports. Eisenkot called on X for a “national emergency government.”

Context

In Israel, a government needs the support of at least 61 of the 120 lawmakers. Eisenkot is a former military chief who leads the Yashar party, AP reports. Neither main group of parties won a majority in the October election, and Arab-led parties won 19 seats, CBS and Fox report. Eisenkot has up to six weeks; Netanyahu stays on as caretaker.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard called the plan “merely a wordplay,” CBS reports.

Still unknown: How the task force will reach its goal, or whether military force is involved, CBS reports.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
