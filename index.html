Justice Department asks Supreme Court to set aside California ballots

The department asked the Supreme Court on Monday to make California set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 people on a government list of suspected noncitizens. A federal judge and then an appeals court, 2-1, refused. The department says the ballots cannot be separated once they are mixed with others. California says the list is unreliable and likely includes many citizens.

Supreme Court hears Colorado preschool case Tuesday

The justices hear arguments Tuesday on whether Colorado can keep Catholic preschools out of its state-funded preschool program. The schools turn away families whose beliefs on marriage and sex differ from church teaching, and say the state allows other exceptions. Colorado says religious schools may teach their faith but must give all families an equal chance to enroll. Lower courts sided with Colorado.

A federal magistrate judge on Monday dismissed a complaint accusing former FBI officials Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides of lying to Congress about the Hunter Biden laptop investigation. How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
