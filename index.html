Kilmar Ábrego García leaves the U.S. for Spain, and a judge closes his immigration case

Kilmar Ábrego García, the Salvadoran man the U.S. mistakenly deported in 2025, flew out of Washington Dulles Airport on Tuesday, AP reports, and landed in Madrid on Wednesday, AP and Fox report. U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis dismissed his immigration case Wednesday, AP reports. His lawyer called it “a temporary departure.” The Homeland Security Department says he left under a removal order and will not be allowed back.

Context

Ábrego García came to the U.S. illegally as a teenager. In 2019 an immigration judge barred sending him to El Salvador because of gang threats against his family, AP reports. He was sent to a Salvadoran prison in March 2025, then brought back after a judge ordered it. Smuggling charges against him were dismissed in May. The administration calls him a gang member, which he has repeatedly denied, AP reports.

Trump pardons two Thanksgiving turkeys, Freedom and Feathers

President Trump pardoned two turkeys, Freedom and Feathers, at the White House on Tuesday, CBS and Fox report. It was the 79th National Thanksgiving Turkey Presentation. Feathers briefly ran into the bushes but was pardoned too. Both birds will retire at Texas A&M University. Trump paused the ceremony to thank troops enforcing the naval blockade of Iran, Fox reports.

CBSFox

Context

Presidents have been presented turkeys since Harry Truman in 1947, and the pardon became a tradition in 1989 under George H.W. Bush, CBS reports. The birds were raised by a farm family in West Virginia picked by the National Turkey Federation. A naval blockade uses warships to stop ships from going in or out of a country’s ports.

U.S. News

The Army detonates its stockpile of unstable lead azide in Iowa

The Army detonated about 5 tons of unstable lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown on Wednesday morning, AP and CBS report. The EPA said its air monitors found no airborne lead in the first two hours afterward, CBS reports. Maj. Gen. Eric Shirley said the risk was reduced as close to zero as humanly possible. Iowa Gov.-elect Rob Sand called for immediate transparency, AP reports.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

Lead azide is a chemical used in detonators, the small charges that set off bigger ones. Inspectors found in March that it had partly crystallized, so it could explode on its own, AP reports. Residents and environmental groups feared lead dust, which is especially harmful to children, could spread. A federal appeals court let the plan go ahead on Monday, saying an uncontrolled explosion posed greater harm, CBS reports.

Storm moves Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday

Macy’s moved its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York from Thursday to Saturday at 8:30 a.m. because of a strong coastal storm, AP and CBS report. More than 80 million people from South Carolina to Massachusetts were under weather alerts Wednesday, with gusts up to 70 mph possible, CBS reports. New York Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency for New York City and nearby counties.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

Organizers had worried all week about how the giant helium balloons would handle wind between skyscrapers, AP reports. CBS says it is the parade’s first postponement in its 100-year history, though it was not held from 1942 to 1944 during World War II. NBC planned to air a rerun of the 2025 parade on Thursday. Parts of Illinois and Missouri, including St. Louis, were under winter storm warnings, CBS reports.

Regular gas in Kansas averaged $3.35 a gallon on Wednesday, according to AAA figures reported by WIBW. That is 18 cents lower than a week earlier and 59 cents lower than a month ago, but 66 cents higher than a year ago. The national average was $3.58. Kansas diesel averaged $5.30, down 77 cents in a month.

Leaked records point to at least 490,000 Russian soldiers dead in the Ukraine war

Leaked Russian government records show at least 490,000 Russian soldiers have died since Russia invaded Ukraine in 2022, according to analyses reported by CBS and Fox. Mediazona, an independent Russian outlet, puts the likely total near 700,000, CBS reports. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Wednesday that only Russia’s Defense Ministry can officially disclose casualty figures.

CBSFox

Context

The records were obtained by pro-Ukraine hacking groups, the Belarusian Cyber Partisans and Silent Crow, CBS reports. Fox says they came from Rosstat, Russia’s statistics agency. In May, the head of GCHQ, Britain’s electronic spy agency, cited about 500,000 Russian military deaths, CBS reports.

Business

Trump administration proposes new tariffs on China, the EU and others

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs on Wednesday, including an additional 20% on Chinese goods, AP and CBS report. Vietnam would face 20%, and the European Union, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and eight other trading partners would face 10% to 18%, AP reports. Brazil’s sugar and aircraft were also targeted. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told CNBC none would likely take effect for a couple of months.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

A tariff is a tax on imported goods, paid by the company bringing them in. In February, the Supreme Court ruled Trump could not use an economic emergency to impose broad tariffs, AP reports. These use a different law, Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. China’s Foreign Ministry opposed the plan. In a post-midterm CBS News poll, 66% said Trump’s tariffs have hurt the economy.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
