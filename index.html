Supreme Court lets border wall work resume in Big Bend

The Supreme Court on Monday let the Trump administration resume building a border wall in the Big Bend area of West Texas while a lawsuit continues, CBS and Fox report. Its unsigned order lifted a lower court’s order that had halted the work. Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson said she would have denied the government’s request, both outlets report.

CBSFox

Context

Kansas

Kansas and Wyandotte County settle their fight over the Chiefs stadium

Gov. Laura Kelly and Mayor Christal Watson of Kansas City, Kansas, and Wyandotte County announced an agreement Monday that ends the county’s lawsuit over the planned Chiefs stadium, KCUR and KAKE report. The county will drop the suit once the deal is fully signed. Watson gets a voting seat on the state board overseeing the stadium, and the Quindaro Ruins area leaves the state’s tax district, the outlets report.

KCURKAKE

Context

The Chiefs plan to move to a $3 billion domed stadium in Kansas City, Kansas, paid for partly with STAR bonds, which are repaid with sales taxes from the area around a project. The county sued in October, saying a state law took away local powers. On Nov. 5, county leaders voted to pledge local tax money from the stadium area, KCUR reports.

Russia’s best-known independent newspaper closes its office

Novaya Gazeta, Russia’s most prominent independent newspaper, closed its Moscow office Monday, AP reports. The paper announced Oct. 30 that it would stop work on Nov. 9, saying Russian authorities and security services forced it to, CBS and AP report. Editor-in-chief Sergei Sokolov said he believes the paper will return someday, AP reports.

PBS (AP)CBS (Oct. 30)

Context

Novaya Gazeta was founded in 1993 and became known for reporting on corruption and human rights abuses. Its former editor, Dmitry Muratov, shared the 2021 Nobel Peace Prize. How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
