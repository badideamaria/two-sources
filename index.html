Politics

Supreme Court will hear the case over Trump’s border asylum ban

The Supreme Court said Friday it will decide whether President Trump can bar migrants who cross the southern border illegally from seeking asylum, AP and CBS report. Lower courts ruled the policy illegal, but it has stayed in effect while the case goes on. A ruling is expected around the end of June, both report.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

Asylum is protection a country gives people who fear harm or persecution in their home country. Trump issued the order on his first day back in office, calling the border situation an “invasion,” CBS reports. Solicitor General D. John Sauer wrote that border encounters have fallen 93% since it took effect, AP reports. Challengers, including ACLU lawyers, say crossings were already falling before Trump took office, AP reports.

U.S. News

Nationwide ground stop halts U.S. flights for more than four hours

The FAA stopped flights at all U.S. airports Friday afternoon after a communications failure, then lifted the order more than four hours later, AP, CBS and Fox report. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a router at a remote site overloaded the air traffic control system while a contractor replaced copper wire with fiber. Officials saw no sign of a cyberattack. FlightAware counted more than 5,000 cancellations Saturday, CBS reports.

PBS (AP)CBSFox (live updates)

Context

A ground stop orders planes to stay on the ground. CBS says it was the first nationwide one since January 2023, when an FAA computer system failed. Officials expected airlines to be back on schedule by the end of Sunday, AP and CBS report. Duffy has asked Congress for more money to update the system, which he called “incredibly old,” CBS reports.

Tanker hits a docked river cruise boat in New Orleans, and all aboard survive

The tanker Atlantic Asphalt struck the docked river cruise boat American Heritage on the Mississippi River in New Orleans on Friday, AP and CBS report. The cruise boat flipped onto its side, and about 100 people were pulled from the river, CBS reports, citing the mayor. Officials said everyone was accounted for and no deaths were reported. Thirty-two people went to hospitals.

Still unknown: What caused the crash. The Coast Guard said the tanker seemed to lose steering, and federal investigators are looking into it.

Reports differ: How many were aboard. AP quotes Mayor Helena Moreno as saying 180. CBS quotes her as saying 191 passengers and crew.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

The American Heritage is a five-story paddlewheel boat that holds up to 150 passengers, CBS reports, and it was finishing an eight-day river cruise, AP reports. The National Transportation Safety Board, or NTSB, investigates transportation crashes. Its team at first could not reach New Orleans because flights were grounded, AP reports. About 16,000 gallons of fuel remain on the tanker, CBS reports.

Science & Health

U.S. has lost its measles elimination status, reports say

The Pan American Health Organization has decided the U.S. no longer meets the standard for having eliminated measles, CBS and Fox report. CBS cites researchers who spoke to its partner KFF Health News; Fox cites a Wall Street Journal report. The U.S. had held the status since 2000. The CDC counts 5,412 cases this year, more than double last year’s total. A public announcement is expected Tuesday.

CBSFox (citing WSJ)

Context

Measles is a very contagious virus that a vaccine can prevent. A country counts as having eliminated it when no outbreak spreads locally for 12 months or longer. The health organization says the best way to keep that status is a vaccination rate of 95% or more, CBS reports. Of this year’s cases, 96% were in people unvaccinated or of unknown status, CBS reports.

Flu season gets an early start in the South and West

Seven states had high flu activity in the week ending Nov. 7, up from two the week before, AP reports, citing the CDC’s Friday update. They are California, Louisiana, Oregon, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas and Washington. The CDC estimates at least 2.6 million flu illnesses and 36,000 hospitalizations so far this season. Most cases are a type called A(H1N1).

PBS (AP)CDC

Context

Flu is a virus that spreads more in colder months. Activity is higher than usual for this point in the year, but it is unclear whether the season will be worse than others, AP reports. Andrew Pekosz of Johns Hopkins told AP early data suggest this year’s vaccine works against the main strain. Doctors expect faster spread around Thanksgiving travel, AP reports.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
