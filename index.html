Kansas schools must write rules for classroom technology, including AI

The Kansas State Board of Education approved a policy last week requiring every school district and accredited private school to adopt rules on classroom technology by July 1, 2027, KWCH reports. The rules must cover how students use technology, digital citizenship and well-being, artificial intelligence, and privacy and security. Local school boards decide how to meet those expectations.

Context

The board said it stresses “meaningful interactions between teachers and students.”

Science & Health

Congo’s Ebola outbreak passes 11,000 confirmed cases

Six months after it was declared, the Ebola outbreak in Congo is still spreading, AP reports. The CDC counts 11,254 confirmed cases and 5,505 confirmed deaths in Congo as of Nov. 12, and calls it the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. Cases have also been confirmed in Uganda, Kenya, Nigeria and France, the CDC says. It rates the risk to Americans as low.

PBS (AP)CDC

Context

Ebola is a virus that causes severe illness and spreads through contact with a sick person’s body fluids. The outbreak has reached eight of Congo’s 26 provinces, AP reports. Health workers have gone on strike over pay that is months overdue, and experts told AP there is little chance of bringing it under control within the next year. No U.S. cases are linked to it, the CDC says.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
