Politics

Rep. Jimmy Gomez will resign after a House ethics report on sexual misconduct

The House Ethics Committee released a report Monday finding “substantial evidence” that Rep. Jimmy Gomez, a California Democrat, made unwelcome sexual advances toward a House staffer, AP and CBS report. Gomez said he will resign Dec. 1. He said he disagrees with the report but takes responsibility for personal mistakes outside his marriage. The committee did not recommend a punishment.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

The Ethics Committee is a group of House members from both parties that looks into lawmakers’ conduct. Gomez has said the conduct was consensual, AP reports. He lost his reelection race this month to fellow Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres and was already set to leave. The committee left any punishment to the full House, CBS reports. House Democratic leaders called the report “serious and deeply troubling.”

Appeals court gives Trump a new chance to move his hush money case to federal court

A three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled Monday that a lower-court judge wrongly refused to move President Trump’s New York hush money case to federal court, AP reports. It ordered a federal court to decide promptly whether the case belongs there, and paused Trump’s separate appeal in state court. The Manhattan district attorney’s office declined to comment.

Context

U.S. News

Appeals court lets the Army go ahead with detonating old explosives in Iowa

A federal appeals court on Monday declined to block the Army from detonating degraded explosives containing lead azide at an ammunition plant near Middletown, Iowa, CBS and Fox report. Earlier Monday, President Trump said it would not happen unless the court and Iowa Gov.-elect Rob Sand agreed. Sand neither approved nor rejected it, Fox reports. The Army plans the detonation Wednesday, both report.

CBSFox

Context

Lead azide is a chemical used in detonators that can go off easily. It was stored for decades and has deteriorated, CBS reports, and the Army says cold weather makes an accidental explosion more likely. Environmental groups that sued warn lead dust could spread 100 miles or more, CBS reports. Sand, a Democrat, is not yet in office, so his approval is not legally required, CBS reports.

World News

U.S. adds sanctions on networks it says move money for Iran

The Treasury Department sanctioned about two dozen Iranian exchange houses, front companies and people on Monday, CBS and Fox report. Treasury says they help Iran move money through Dubai, Hong Kong and Turkey, Fox reports. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Iran’s “economy is underwater.”

FoxCBS (live updates, with AP)

Context

Sanctions are penalties: any U.S. assets of those named are frozen, and Americans can’t do business with them, Fox reports. Exchange houses swap one currency for another. The U.S. keeps a naval blockade of Iranian ports. Iran says the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow sea route for oil, could reopen within two days if the U.S. accepts its demands, including lifting the blockade, CBS reports.

Nvidia’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive Monday, less than two weeks after he resigned, CBS and Fox report. The board said input from senior U.S. officials gave it confidence his return poses no national security risk. Nvidia shares closed up about 31%, Fox reports. CBSFox (live updates)

Context

Science & Health

Nearly 150 people fall ill on a cruise ship that returned to Florida Monday

At least 134 passengers and 14 crew members on Princess Cruises’ Caribbean Princess got sick with symptoms including vomiting and diarrhea during a 12-night voyage, CBS and the CDC report. The ship returned to Port Everglades in Fort Lauderdale on Monday. The CDC lists norovirus as the cause but says samples are still awaiting confirmation tests.

CBS MiamiCDC outbreak page

Context

Norovirus is a very contagious stomach bug. Cruise ships must report cases of stomach illness to the CDC, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, which tracks outbreaks. The ship carried 2,936 passengers and 1,132 crew, CBS reports. Princess Cruises said its crew stepped up cleaning, and passengers told CBS the buffet was closed.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
