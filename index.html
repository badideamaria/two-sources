Supreme Court turns down California ballot request

The Justice Department’s emergency request to make California set aside mail ballots from about 20,000 people the government says are noncitizens was denied Monday. A federal judge and an appeals court had already refused. California says the federal list includes citizens.

Reports differ: Who acted. CBS reports a one-line, unsigned order from the court with no noted dissents. Fox reports Justice Elena Kagan denied it alone, without sending it to the other justices.

CBSFox

Context

States run their own elections, and California mails a ballot to every registered voter. Only citizens can vote in federal elections. The Trump administration says people on its list aren’t citizens and wants their ballots kept apart until that’s checked. California officials say the list has mistakes, so real citizens could lose their votes.

Judge dismisses suit over Alaska Senate results

U.S. District Judge Sharon Gleason dismissed a Justice Department lawsuit that sought to block Alaska from certifying its Senate results if Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan loses. CBS reports the department has filed notice of an appeal.

CBSPBS

Context

When a state “certifies” an election, it officially confirms who won. The Justice Department argued a second candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan was added to the ballot to confuse voters. Democrat Mary Peltola, who is running against Sen. Sullivan, denies coordinating with him. To bring a case, you need “standing,” a real legal stake, and the judge ruled the department didn’t have it.

Judge throws out case against two ex-FBI officials

Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui dismissed charges Monday that former FBI officials Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides lied to Congress in 2023 about the Hunter Biden laptop investigation. He found no probable cause and called the case “weaponization.” The Justice Department told CBS it disagrees and is weighing its options, including seeking a grand jury indictment.

CBSPBS (AP)Fox

Context

Lying to Congress is a federal crime. A “criminal complaint” is a quick way to file charges; an indictment comes from a grand jury, a panel of citizens who review the evidence. “Probable cause” means enough evidence to reasonably believe a crime happened. Hunter Biden is former President Joe Biden’s son, and how the FBI handled his laptop has been argued about for years.

President Trump gave his last speech before the election Monday at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool, which he declared reopened after a costly repair. AP reports he spent much of it on the project and again blamed vandalism for its peeling liner. Later, on Fox News, he said “If our people vote, we win in a landslide.”

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

The Reflecting Pool is the long pool in front of the Lincoln Memorial in Washington, D.C. Trump’s repair project ran months late and far over his early cost estimate. He blames vandals. Prosecutors dropped their case against a former Olympian and said the problems came from “shoddy workmanship.” The midterms are national elections held halfway through a president’s four-year term.

U.S. News

Florida jet may have broken apart in the air

Federal investigators said Monday there are early signs the Learjet that crashed Sunday in Palm Beach County was breaking up before it hit the ground. NTSB member Tom Chapman called weather “a major focus.” The crew had reported ice and turbulence at 35,000 feet and asked to climb, then declared an emergency. All nine aboard died.

Still unknown: The cause. The NTSB says its work at the site will take several days.

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

Planes flying high can collect ice, which makes them harder to control. The NTSB, or National Transportation Safety Board, is the federal agency that investigates crashes. “Breaking up” means parts may have come off in the air, and one clue is finding pieces far from where the plane hit. Final reports usually take a year or more.

U.S. strikes Iranian island after fire on a U.S. aircraft

U.S. forces carried out what officials called limited, defensive strikes Monday evening on Iran’s Qeshm Island near the Strait of Hormuz. A senior U.S. official told CBS they followed Revolutionary Guard fire at an American aircraft. Iranian state media reported explosions on the island. Earlier Monday, President Trump wrote that a deal with Iran “could happen.”

Still unknown: Damage, casualties and Iran’s official response. None were reported when I checked.

CBSPBS (AP)Fox

Context

The U.S. and Iran are in a war that talks haven’t ended. The Strait of Hormuz is a narrow waterway next to Iran, and about a fifth of the world’s oil normally passes through it, so trouble there can raise gas prices everywhere. “Defensive” is how U.S. officials describe it: answering an attack, not starting a new round of fighting.

Hackers apparently overheat supermarket freezers in Europe

Fridges and freezers malfunctioned overnight at stores in Sweden, Belgium, Luxembourg and Estonia, and some food was thrown away. A Swedish store and an Estonian equipment maker said attackers set temperatures to 50°C (122°F). Delhaize reported several dozen stores affected. No one has claimed responsibility.

PBS (AP)CBS (AFP)

Context

Big store fridges are often run by computers connected to the internet, so they can be checked remotely. If that software is out of date or poorly protected, outsiders can break in. In October, a similar attack hit hundreds of supermarkets in France. Investigators there linked it to older equipment that no longer got security updates.

U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff said Monday on X that the U.S. will mediate working-level talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11. He said they will cover security after the war, de-escalation and humanitarian issues.

One source: Only Fox had this when I checked, and it does not say whether Russia or Ukraine confirmed they will attend.

Fox

Context

Russia invaded Ukraine in February 2022, and the war is still going. “Working-level” talks are between negotiators rather than presidents, usually to sort out details. To “mediate” means to sit between two sides and help them reach a deal. Abu Dhabi is the capital of the United Arab Emirates.
