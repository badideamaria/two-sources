Politics

Peltola wins Alaska’s Senate race, giving Democrats 56 seats

Democrat Mary Peltola defeated Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan in Alaska, according to projections Tuesday night by CBS News and Fox News. AP also called the race, Fox reports. Democrats will hold a 56-44 majority in January, CBS and Fox report.

Reports differ: Whether ranked-choice counting decided it. Fox says Peltola won 50.1%, so no ranked-choice count was needed. CBS says the contest was decided by ranked-choice voting.

CBSFoxFox (results)

Context

In ranked-choice voting, voters rank candidates; if no one wins a majority, the last-place candidate is dropped and those ballots move to the next choice. A second candidate also named Dan Sullivan was on the ballot. Republicans argued that could hurt Sen. Sullivan, CBS reports, and Peltola’s campaign denied any role in it, Fox reports. Peltola is set to be the first Alaska Native elected to the Senate, CBS reports.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
