Senate rejects a House-passed measure to end the Iran war

The Senate voted 48-51 Thursday against a resolution directing the president to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran, CBS and AP report. Republicans Susan Collins, Lisa Murkowski and Rand Paul voted yes. Democrat John Fetterman and Republican Thom Tillis voted no.

Context

Kansas

Schwab questions who paid for a partial U.S. Senate recount

Kansas Secretary of State Scott Schwab says the $47,000 to pay for a recount requested by Libertarian Senate candidate David Graham came from an account of the Show Me the Votes Foundation, not Graham’s campaign, WIBW reports. Schwab said that raises “serious concerns” about federal campaign finance rules and is referring it to the FEC and the Justice Department. It covers 60 precincts in Shawnee, Johnson and Wyandotte counties.

Still unknown: WIBW reports no response from Graham or the foundation.

One source: Only WIBW’s report was found. KWCH, KCUR and KAKE had no story on the recount.

WIBW

Context

In a recount, ballots are counted again. In Kansas, a candidate can request one and pay its cost. Schwab said Graham told his office the recount was to help a researcher with academic work. The FEC, or Federal Election Commission, enforces federal rules on who can give money to campaigns and how much.

World News

UN’s top court rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled 9-6 Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017, AP and PBS report. It is the first time the court has found a country responsible for genocide. AP reports Myanmar was ordered to allow Rohingya to return home safely, prosecute those responsible and pay compensation, with the amount set later. Myanmar’s government had not responded.

PBS (AP)CBS (AP)PBS NewsHour

Context

The Rohingya are a mostly Muslim minority in Myanmar. Gambia brought the case in 2019 under the 1948 Genocide Convention. The court’s rulings are binding, but it can only report a country that ignores them to the UN Security Council, AP reports. About 1.2 million Rohingya refugees live in Bangladesh. Britain said it was expelling Myanmar’s defense attaché, AP and PBS report.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
