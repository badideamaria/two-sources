Justice Department civil rights chief Harmeet Dhillon resigns

Harmeet Dhillon resigned Thursday as head of the Justice Department’s Civil Rights Division, CBS and PBS report. Attorney General Todd Blanche announced it, CBS reports. In her letter, Dhillon said she told the White House in early October she would leave after the midterms. Two sources told CBS that Trump had demanded she be fired in October. The White House said she did a “fantastic job.”

CBSPBS

Context

The Civil Rights Division enforces laws against discrimination and protects voting rights. Under Dhillon, PBS reports, it sued universities, dropped police reform efforts and pressed states for voter lists. CBS reports more than 75% of its staff left last year, and the department has lost 27 of 31 lawsuits seeking states’ voter rolls. CBS says no replacement has been named.

U.S. News

Newsom to commute all 548 California death sentences

California Gov. Gavin Newsom said Thursday he will change the sentences of all 548 people on the state’s death row to life in prison without parole, AP and Fox report. Newsom called the death penalty incompatible with California’s values. Los Angeles County District Attorney Nathan Hochman said only lawmakers or voters can end it, Fox reports. The ACLU praised the move, AP reports.

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

To commute a sentence is to replace it with a lighter one. The death penalty stays law in California. The state has not executed anyone since 2006, Fox reports, and Newsom paused executions in 2019. Voters rejected repealing the death penalty in 2016. AP reports the state Supreme Court must approve clemency for anyone convicted of more than one felony. Newsom is leaving office after eight years.

Emporia and El Dorado voters ban large data centers

Voters in Emporia and El Dorado approved bans on “high-impact” data centers Tuesday, KAKE and WIBW report. Emporia’s passed 4,346 to 2,891, WIBW reports. KAKE reports El Dorado’s ban lasts 10 years, and Emporia’s can’t take effect until a Lyon County judge rules, because it cites a section of city code that doesn’t exist.

Data centers are buildings full of computers that run websites and artificial intelligence, and they can use large amounts of electricity and water. In El Dorado, KAKE reports, residents petitioned after learning in the spring that the city was pursuing the industry.

Science & Health

Many babies diagnosed with peanut allergy may not have one

About 35% of infants who had reacted to peanuts and tested positive showed no reaction when fed small, rising amounts under medical supervision, an Australian study found, CBS reports. Those babies had the allergy label removed. Researcher Kirsten Perrett says such a supervised test should be routinely considered before starting treatment. Dr. Tania Elliott told CBS parents should never try it at home.

One source: Only CBS had this study when I checked.

CBS

Context

The supervised test is called an oral food challenge. Most parents rely on allergy tests alone, which CBS reports often weren’t enough to show a real allergy. About 1 in 50 U.S. children has a peanut allergy, CBS reports. Dr. Tania Elliott, a CBS medical contributor, said 12% of those babies had anaphylaxis, a severe reaction, which is why the challenge must be done by doctors.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
