Politics

Supreme Court leaves Maryland gun limits and a UC grants ruling in place

The Supreme Court on Monday declined to hear a challenge to Maryland laws banning guns in “sensitive places” such as schools and public transit, CBS and PBS report. It also turned away the Trump administration’s appeal over canceled research grants at the University of California, leaving in place a lower-court ruling that the cuts likely violated researchers’ free speech rights.

CBS (guns)CBS (grants)PBS (News Wrap)

Context

Declining to hear a case leaves the lower court’s ruling standing without the Supreme Court deciding the issue itself. Justice Clarence Thomas agreed with turning the gun case away but wrote that he doubts many such limits, CBS reports. The grants, from two federal science and humanities agencies, were canceled over the administration’s objections to diversity programs. The administration says about $59 million remains on them, CBS reports.

Customers of JPMorgan Chase, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank and other banks reported trouble Monday reaching accounts online and making transfers, CBS and Fox report. Chase said it was a technical bug, not a cyberattack. Fox reports the problem was traced to a software update Chase put out earlier Monday. Context

More Kansas households turn to the Kansas Food Bank

Households served by Kansas Food Bank programs rose 88% from last year, according to the food bank’s data, the Kansas News Service reports. KWCH reports the food bank is serving about 7,000 more households than a year ago, while donations are down about 20%. CEO Brian Walker points to higher grocery and gas prices, the Kansas News Service reports.

Context

The Kansas Food Bank supplies food pantries, soup kitchens and other programs in 85 Kansas counties. Walker says older adults and rural residents are hit hardest, the Kansas News Service reports. Its fuel costs for deliveries are up 51%, KWCH reports. SNAP, the federal food aid program once called food stamps, had its funding cut last year and added work requirements.

U.S. moves to stop China from renting advanced AI chips abroad

The Commerce Department issued a rule Monday aimed at keeping advanced AI chips from reaching China through other countries, CBS and PBS report. It requires licenses to send advanced chips to Brazil, Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand. It also targets remote access, where users rent computing power from data centers in countries not covered by U.S. export limits.

CBSPBS

Context

AI chips are powerful processors used to build artificial intelligence. How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
