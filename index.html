U.S. News

A Delta computer problem delays more than half its flights during Thanksgiving travel

A Delta system that calculates whether planes are too heavy to fly malfunctioned Sunday during busy Thanksgiving travel, AP, CBS and Fox report. By mid-afternoon, 67% of Delta’s flights were delayed, AP reports, citing FlightAware. By evening, Delta had canceled 202 flights, CBS reports. Delta said the system was working again but operations were not yet back to normal.

PBS (AP)CBSFox

Context

Before takeoff, crews must check a plane’s weight and balance, meaning how heavy it is and how that weight is spread out. Workers did the math by hand while the system was down, Fox reports. The FAA briefly stopped some flights into Atlanta, Delta’s hub, and said its systems were not the cause. Delta said about 70% of delays came from the system problem and the rest from weather.

Kansas

Kansas Rep. Derek Schmidt’s bill on police background checks becomes law

President Trump signed a bill by Kansas Rep. Derek Schmidt that lets state agencies that certify police officers get criminal history records directly from the FBI, KWCH and WIBW report. Before, those agencies relied on the departments doing the hiring to run the checks. Schmidt says the law “closes a gap in law enforcement background checks.”

One source: KWCH and WIBW are sister stations running the same story, based mostly on Schmidt’s announcement. It does not give the signing date.

KWCHWIBW

Context

In Kansas, police officers must be certified by a state agency, the Kansas Commission on Peace Officer Standards and Training, which Schmidt worked with on the bill. A criminal history record lists a person’s past arrests and convictions. Schmidt, a Republican, represents Kansas’s 2nd District. Democratic Rep. Deborah Ross of North Carolina introduced the bill, called the Criminal History Access Act, with him.

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended Sunday night without its main agreement after running past its deadline, AP and CBS report. The European Union and other countries rejected a final draft that did not mention fossil fuels, both report. Negotiators made progress on some smaller issues, including adapting to climate change, AP reports.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

COP31 is this year’s meeting where nearly 200 countries negotiate on climate change. Deals need everyone to agree, so a few countries can block one. The U.S. sent no one for a second year, CBS reports. Next year’s talks are in Ethiopia.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
