Judge sets limits on federal poll monitors

U.S. District Judge Reggie Walton denied the NAACP’s request to block the Justice Department’s Election Day poll monitors. But he ordered the department to make sure its monitors are unarmed clerical or administrative staff, not law enforcement, and don’t wear agency uniforms or talk with voters inside polling places.

One source: Only Fox had this when I checked.

Fox

Context

Poll monitors watch voting to see that federal voting laws are followed. The NAACP is a civil rights group. Fox reports it sued after allegations the department planned to send more than 1,000 people, including agents from agencies like the DEA and ATF, and argued that could scare voters. Department lawyers told the court sworn agents would be left out.

U.S. News

Floods force evacuations across the Southwest

Heavy rain brought flash flooding and evacuations to parts of Colorado, Arizona and New Mexico on Sunday. Forecasters expected the San Juan River in Pagosa Springs, Colorado, to crest near 22 feet, above its 1911 record. Durango also ordered evacuations. AP reports helicopter rooftop rescues in Sedona, Arizona.

Still unknown: How high the San Juan River finally rose. AP said it was about 14 feet at sundown Sunday.

PBS (AP)CBS Colorado

Context

To “crest” means a river reaches its highest point before it starts to drop. Pagosa Springs flooded about a week earlier too, so the ground was already soaked, and CBS Colorado notes that sends rain running straight into rivers. The Southwest is usually dry, and a National Weather Service meteorologist told AP the Tucson area got two or three months of rain in a couple of days.

Iran warns it may respond to U.S. strikes

After Monday’s U.S. strikes on Qeshm Island, Iran’s deputy foreign minister said Tuesday that Tehran keeps the right to self-defense, CBS reports. Qatar’s foreign ministry spokesman said Qatar is still hopeful about a temporary U.S.-Iran deal. Fox reports Iran’s armed forces chief listed conditions for peace, including war reparations and recognition of Iran’s control over the Strait of Hormuz.

Still unknown: Damage or casualties on Qeshm. AP and Fox reported none when I checked.

Context

Qeshm is an Iranian island in the Strait of Hormuz. Fox reports it has an underground missile site, a naval base and a large plant that turns seawater into drinking water. Qatar, a small Gulf country, has been hosting talks between the two sides. “Reparations” are payments for damage caused in a war.

Hackers send fake alerts in Ukraine and Russia

Late Sunday, Ukraine’s Air Alert app sent false warnings of nuclear, chemical and radiation threats. Hours later, Russia’s Emergencies Ministry said hackers used its app to send a false alert to 33 regions. Ukraine’s app was later running again. A local truce around Russian-occupied Oleshky, which let some civilians leave, also ended Sunday night.

Still unknown: Who hacked either app. A Russian Telegram channel said a group called Hitcore broke into Ukraine’s, which AP could not verify.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

In wartime, phone apps warn people to take shelter from missiles and drones. Ukraine has been at war with Russia since Russia invaded in 2022. Oleshky is a city in southern Ukraine that Russia controls. The truce there began October 27, and both sides credited U.S. first lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo with helping arrange it.

Man finishes 28-year walk around the world

Karl Bushby, a former British paratrooper, reached his mother’s home in Hull, England, on Monday, ending a walk he began in Chile on November 1, 1998. His rules: no machines to move ahead, and no going home until he finished.

Reports differ: Distance. AP, via PBS, says about 31,000 miles. CBS says about 30,000.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

Bushby walked north through the Americas, crossed the Bering Sea to Russia, then went across Asia and Europe, swimming the Caspian Sea and the English Channel. AP lists delays including visa problems in Russia, jail in Panama, the 2008 financial crash and the COVID-19 pandemic.

Marvell agrees to buy Lumentum for $115 billion

Chipmaker Marvell Technology agreed Monday to buy Lumentum, which makes lasers and switches used in data centers, in an all-stock deal valued at $115 billion. Marvell CEO Matt Murphy said AI data centers are shifting toward optical connections. The deal needs shareholder approval and is expected to close in the first half of 2028.

CBSAxios

Context

In an all-stock deal, the buyer pays with its own shares instead of cash. Optical connections send data as light through glass fiber, which can carry more data than electrical wires. Companies building data centers for artificial intelligence need huge amounts of data moved quickly between chips.

Ebola travel statement starts tonight

After 11:59 p.m. Eastern Tuesday, everyone boarding an international flight to the U.S., including Americans and airline crews, must sign a statement that they haven’t been in Congo in the past 21 days, the CDC says. Airline groups had asked for a delay. Neither the CDC page nor Fox reported one.

CDCFox

Context

Ebola is a deadly virus that spreads through contact with a sick person’s body fluids. Symptoms can take up to 21 days to appear, which is why that number is used. The Democratic Republic of the Congo, in central Africa, has an Ebola outbreak. CDC rules already bar anyone who was there in the past 21 days from boarding.

Washington resident dies of bird flu

A Yakima County, Washington, resident with earlier health problems died Sunday of H5N1 bird flu after exposure at a dairy with infected cattle. AP calls it the first such U.S. death in a year. The CDC found no genetic changes that would help the virus spread more easily and says the risk to the public is low.

One source: Only AP, carried by PBS, had this when I checked.

PBS (AP)

Context

Bird flu mostly infects birds, but since 2024 it has also spread among dairy cows. AP reports more than 70 human infections in the U.S. since 2024, and farm workers are at higher risk. Health officials have found no spread from one person to another, which is the main thing they watch for.

NASA’s Swift telescope is falling back to Earth

NASA’s Swift observatory, which spent 22 years in orbit, is expected to come down Wednesday. NASA puts the chance of anyone being hurt at about 1 in 2,100. A rescue mission meant to raise its orbit failed earlier this year.

One source: Only AP, carried by CBS, had the reentry date when I checked.

CBS (AP)

Context

Swift watched for gamma-ray bursts, the brightest explosions known, and quickly alerted bigger telescopes like Hubble and Webb. Strong activity on the sun heats and puffs up Earth’s upper air, which drags on satellites and pulls them lower.

How this is made
