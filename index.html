Politics

Trump orders federal contractors to check with the White House before answering Congress

An executive order President Trump signed late Friday tells federal contractors and subcontractors to consult his administration about requests from Congress and to withhold communications the White House considers privileged, AP and Fox report. Companies that don’t comply could lose their contracts or be suspended from future federal work, both report.

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

Contractors are private companies paid to do work for the government; AP says the order covers tens of thousands of them. Executive privilege is a president’s claim that some internal talks can stay private, and AP notes it is not absolute. Democrats take control of Congress in January and are expected to start investigations, AP reports. Rep. Jamie Raskin called the order “totally unprecedented and blatantly unlawful,” AP reports.

Trump says he would work with Democrats on a 10% cap on credit card interest

President Trump said Saturday on Fox News that he would work with Democrats, who take control of Congress in January, on capping credit card interest rates at 10%, AP and Fox report. He named the cap, health care and the upcoming debt limit as possible areas for compromise, Fox reports. Sen. Elizabeth Warren replied, “Let’s cap credit card interest rates,” AP reports.

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

Interest is what a lender charges for borrowing money. The median credit card rate is about 22.2%, according to a New York Federal Reserve survey cited by AP. Trump proposed a 10% cap in January, but banks opposed it and the Republican-led Congress made little effort to pass it, AP reports. Banks argue a cap would lead them to cancel cards for some customers or cut rewards.

U.S. News

FBI investigates an apparent hack of a national crime database

The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, a database police use across the country, AP and Fox report, citing unnamed sources. The FBI did not comment. Asked about it on Fox News Saturday, President Trump said, “I will say that it’s not good.”

Still unknown: What information, if any, was affected, both report.

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

The database holds records on wanted fugitives, missing persons, criminal histories and stolen property, AP reports. Local, state and federal officers use it. CNN first reported the hack. In September, the FBI investigated a hack of its job portal, AP and Fox report. AP says there was no immediate sign this was connected to recent tech outages.

Kansas

Former K-State dean named interim president of Kansas State

The Kansas Board of Regents on Wednesday named Marc A. Johnson interim president of Kansas State University, WIBW and KAKE report. Johnson led the University of Nevada, Reno, from 2011 to 2020 and was once K-State’s dean of agriculture. He takes over Jan. 11 while the board searches for a permanent president.

WIBWKAKE

Context

The Board of Regents oversees Kansas’s public universities. K-State President Richard Linton announced in October that he would leave to lead Virginia Tech, both report. Johnson is an agricultural economist and a Kansas native. He starts Dec. 1 as an adviser to the board. An interim president fills the job until a permanent one is chosen.

World News

Mexico captures a cartel leader on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list

Mexican forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” in Guasave, Sinaloa, AP, CBS and Fox report. He was on the FBI’s Ten Most Wanted list, and the U.S. had offered a $5 million reward. Mexico’s government said U.S. intelligence helped. Fighting broke out in Guasave, with burning vehicles, AP and CBS report.

PBS (AP)CBS (AFP)Fox

Context

A cartel is a criminal group that smuggles drugs. Meza Flores is accused of trafficking drugs into the U.S. and was first indicted in 2012, CBS and Fox report. Mexico’s Defense Ministry said four soldiers and seven alleged gunmen were killed, AP and CBS report. President Claudia Sheinbaum’s government faces pressure from Trump to crack down on cartels, AP reports.

UN climate talks in Turkey run past their deadline without a deal

The UN climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, was set to end Friday but went into extra time Saturday with no final agreement, AP and NPR report. The biggest dispute is over fossil fuels. A draft of the final deal did not mention them, AP reports, and big oil producers such as Saudi Arabia and Russia oppose language aimed at them, NPR reports.

Still unknown: Whether a final agreement was reached. NPR’s report is from Saturday afternoon and AP’s was last updated Saturday evening, U.S. time.

PBS (AP)NPR

Context

COP31 is this year’s meeting where nearly 200 countries negotiate on climate change. Fossil fuels are coal, oil and gas, and burning them is the main cause of global warming. Poorer countries also want richer ones to pay more to help them cope with disasters, both report. Any single country can block a deal, NPR reports. The U.S. is not taking part this year.

Kansas City Current win their first NWSL championship

The Kansas City Current beat the Washington Spirit 2-1 Saturday night in Washington, D.C., for their first NWSL title, CBS and KCUR report. Vanessa DiBernardo scored from a corner kick in the 54th minute, and Debinha added a goal in stoppage time. Trinity Rodman scored for Washington in the 98th minute, CBS reports.

CBSKCUR

Context

The NWSL is the top women’s pro soccer league in the U.S. A goal scored straight from a corner kick, without touching another player, is called an “Olimpico.” Stoppage time is extra time added for delays. The Current began playing in 2021 and were the No. 4 seed, CBS reports. Washington reached the final for a third straight year.

Washington beat defending national champion Indiana 27-24 in overtime. Tyler Robles kicked a 46-yard field goal, and Indiana then missed from 37 yards, both report.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
