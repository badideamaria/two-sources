Politics

Judge blocks the FCC’s early review of ABC’s station licenses

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan blocked the Federal Communications Commission’s early review of the licenses of eight ABC-owned TV stations Wednesday, AP and Fox report. She said ABC was likely to win its First Amendment claim, writing, “This is not a close case.” She turned down ABC’s broader request to bar other pressure from the agency. The FCC called the ruling “wrong on the facts and wrong on the law.”

PBS (AP)Fox

Context

The FCC licenses TV stations that broadcast over the air. The First Amendment protects speech and the press from government punishment. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr, a Trump ally, ordered the early review in April, shortly after a Jimmy Kimmel joke angered Trump, who called for Kimmel’s firing, AP reports. ABC and its parent company, Disney, sued in August. The FCC says it expects to win in the end.

Kansas

Federal rural health grants help southeast Kansas hospitals add services

Some rural health systems in southeast Kansas say federal grants have helped them add medical providers and services, the Kansas News Service reports through KCUR. Wilson Medical Center in Neodesha is expanding its clinic and hiring, and Labette Medical Center bought a laser machine to treat kidney stones without surgery. Kansas is getting about $225 million in the program’s second year, KCUR and WIBW report.

One source: Only the Kansas News Service reported on the hospitals. WIBW reported the $225 million award on Nov. 9.

KCUR (Kansas News Service)WIBW: background

Context

The Rural Health Transformation Program is run by the federal Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, and the state health department oversees it. It is meant to soften cuts to Medicaid, the government health program for many low-income people, from a law Trump signed last year, KCUR reports. Gov. Laura Kelly called it the nation’s sixth-largest award. Hiring providers is “a huge, huge challenge,” a Wilson Medical Center official said.

Andy Taylor, Duran Duran’s original guitarist, dies at 65

Andy Taylor, the original guitarist of the English band Duran Duran, has died at 65, his family and bandmates said, AP and Fox report. They said he died at home on Monday after a battle with prostate cancer. He played on hits including “Hungry Like the Wolf,” “The Reflex” and “Rio.”

CBS (AP)Fox

Context

Duran Duran formed in Birmingham, England, in 1978, and its hits were closely tied to the launch of MTV, AP reports. Taylor also formed the band Power Station in 1984. He left Duran Duran in 1986 and returned for the 2004 album “Astronaut,” AP reports. The band entered the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame in 2022, but he was too ill to attend.

President Trump on Thursday accused the Commerce Department of releasing “fake” economic numbers before the Nov. 3 midterms and asked for an immediate investigation, AP and CBS report. On Wednesday the department raised its estimate of third-quarter growth to a 4.3% annual rate, up from 3.4%. He presented no evidence, CBS reports.

PBS (AP)CBS

Context

The Philadelphia Eagles beat the Dallas Cowboys 39-38 on Thanksgiving, AP and Fox report. Dallas led by 21 points in the third quarter. Jake Elliott won it with a 59-yard field goal with 2 seconds left, AP reports. Fox calls it the biggest comeback in NFL Thanksgiving history. Eagles receiver DeVonta Smith had 200 receiving yards.

Context

The Eagles are 4-7 and the Cowboys 5-7.

How this is made

  • Claude, an AI, opens the articles and writes each story in its own words. It can make mistakes, so every story links to its sources.
  • A fact goes in only if two separate outlets report it. If only one does, the story says so. Two outlets running the same wire story count as one.
  • Before an edition goes up, a separate Claude session that didn’t write it rechecks every story against the articles. No person reads it first, so mistakes can still get through.
  • Context notes explain the background in plainer words. Claude writes them from the articles and long-settled facts, and they don’t have their own source links.
  • Claims and opinions get a name on them. Describing words like “stunning” are not allowed.
  • Choosing which stories to include is still a judgment call. That part can’t be fully objective.
  • AP, Reuters, BBC, The Guardian and The Wall Street Journal block Claude’s search tool, so they are only used where another outlet carries their stories.
